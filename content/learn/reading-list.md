## How to use this list

These pages give you a map. The books below give you the depth. You do not need all of them. Read the first two, then select by the area where your reviews in this app are weakest.

## Core

| Book                                  | Authors                  | What it gives you                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Fundamentals of Software Architecture | Mark Richards, Neal Ford | The base of this app: characteristics, styles, trade-offs, and the role. Start here                     |
| Designing Data-Intensive Applications | Martin Kleppmann         | How databases, replication, partitioning, and streams really work. The standard text for the data pages |

## Distributed systems and services

| Book                                  | Authors                                                    | What it gives you                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Software Architecture: The Hard Parts | Neal Ford, Mark Richards, Pramod Sadalage, Zhamak Dehghani | How to split services and data, with sagas and trade-off analysis in detail                                                        |
| Building Microservices                | Sam Newman                                                 | Practical design of services: boundaries, communication, deployment                                                                |
| Release It!                           | Michael Nygard                                             | How systems fail in production, and the patterns that prevent it. The source of "circuit breaker" and "bulkhead" as software terms |

## A reading plan for interviews

This plan takes seven weeks, at about one to two hours each day. It mixes the books, the lessons, and the katas, because reading alone does not prepare you to design under time pressure. Each week has three parts: read, study the lessons, and practice.

| Week | Read                                                                                                   | Lessons                                                                                                                                                                                   | Practice                                                                                                |
| ---- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | Fundamentals: characteristics and the catalog of styles. System Design Interview: the framework        | [Method](/learn/method), [Characteristics](/learn/characteristics), [Styles](/learn/styles), [Trade-offs](/learn/trade-offs), [Interview format](/learn/interview-format)                 | [Lunch Rush](/kata/lunch-rush). Then [Link Shortener](/kata/link-shortener), with no time limit         |
| 2    | System Design Interview: estimation and scale from one user to millions. DDIA: storage and retrieval   | [Estimation](/learn/estimation), [Scale step by step](/learn/scale-step-by-step), [Storage engines](/learn/storage-engines), [Data stores](/learn/data-stores), [Caching](/learn/caching) | [URL shortener](/learn/url-shortener) case. [Link Shortener](/kata/link-shortener) again, in 45 minutes |
| 3    | DDIA: replication, partitioning, and transactions                                                      | [Consistency](/learn/consistency), [Scaling data](/learn/scaling-data), [Transactions and isolation](/learn/transactions-isolation), [Consistent hashing](/learn/consistent-hashing)      | [Key-value store](/learn/key-value-store) case. [News Feed](/kata/news-feed)                            |
| 4    | DDIA: the trouble with distributed systems, consistency and consensus, streams                         | [Clocks and failures](/learn/clocks-and-failures), [Consensus](/learn/consensus), [Batch and stream](/learn/batch-and-stream), [Messaging](/learn/messaging)                              | [Chat system](/learn/chat-system) case. [Chat Messaging](/kata/chat-messaging)                          |
| 5    | Release It!: stability patterns and antipatterns. SRE: service level objectives and cascading failures | [Resilience](/learn/resilience), [Stability antipatterns](/learn/stability-antipatterns), [Observability](/learn/observability), [Rate limiter](/learn/rate-limiter)                      | [Notification Service](/kata/notification-service). [Agent Triggers](/kata/agent-triggers)              |
| 6    | Hard Parts: data ownership and sagas. Building Microservices: communication and workflow               | [Distributed transactions](/learn/distributed-transactions), [Data ownership](/learn/data-ownership), [Sync and async](/learn/sync-async), [Payment system](/learn/payment-system)        | [Payment System](/kata/payment-system). | 7    | Hohpe: the architect role and communication. Team Topologies: team types                               | [Diagrams](/learn/diagrams), [Teams](/learn/teams), [Evolution](/learn/evolution), [Decision records](/learn/decision-records)                                                            | [Video Streaming](/kata/video-streaming) in 45 minutes. [Waiting Room](/kata/waiting-room)              |

Rules for the plan:

1. Do the kata before you read the case page for it. The gaps that you find are the parts to read with most care.
2. Read the review of each kata. Write down the two weakest points, and add the lessons for them to the next week.
3. Practice aloud from week 3. Explain the design to a friend or a recording, as in a real interview.
4. In the last week, do one interview kata each day in 45 minutes, with no notes.

Change the plan to your gaps:

- **For a role with much domain modeling**, add Domain-Driven Design in week 6, the part on bounded contexts, and the [Bounded contexts](/learn/bounded-contexts) lesson.
- **For a role near the code**, add A Philosophy of Software Design. It is short, and two evenings are enough. Use it with [Deep modules](/learn/deep-modules).
- **For a staff or principal role**, add Accelerate and the [Delivery performance](/learn/delivery-performance) lesson in week 7. Expect questions on how you measure the effect of an architecture.
- **With only six weeks**, join weeks 3 and 4. Read only the parts on replication, partitioning, and consensus.
- **With eight weeks**, use the extra week for the remaining cases: [Web crawler](/learn/web-crawler), [Autocomplete](/learn/autocomplete), [File sync](/learn/file-sync), and [Unique IDs](/learn/unique-ids).
